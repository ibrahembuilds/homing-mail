Staying out of spam

1. Prove the mail is yours

Every sending domain needs SPF, DKIM and DMARC records. Google and Yahoo refuse or junk mail without them. Google Workspace and Zoho give you the DKIM key in their admin panel; for this server, use Domain & DNS.

2. Send from a look-alike domain

Buy a second domain (for example getacme.com next to acme.com) for cold email, so your main domain's reputation is never at risk. Point its website to your main site.

3. Warm up and stay small

New mailboxes start at 10–20 emails a day and grow over 2–4 weeks. Keep each mailbox under 30–50 a day; for more volume add mailboxes, not emails per mailbox.

4. Clean lists, plain emails

Verify addresses before sending (Find & verify): over 2% bounces hurts. Write short, plain text with at most one link, no images or attachments, and leave click tracking off.

5. Watch complaints

Gmail's limit is 0.3% spam reports; aim under 0.1%. Every email here carries a one-click unsubscribe. Sign up your domain at Google Postmaster Tools to see your reputation.

About free addresses

Free Gmail, Outlook and Yahoo addresses can be connected (Gmail allows 500 emails a day), but providers watch them closely and they look less professional. Fine for testing and warm contacts; for outreach use a domain you own. Free domains (.tk, .ml and similar) are mostly blocked, so avoid them.
